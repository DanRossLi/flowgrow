# Flowgrow

An ecology and nature building game on a hex grid, made for a phone. Tiles arrive in a pile and you place them on an island near your river. Two habitats of the same level combine into the next level, and river tiles let you place tiles further from the river. It began on Nov 19, 2026 as a test inside [Flow Tile](https://github.com/DanRossLi/flowtile) and has had its own repo since Nov 20.

**Play:** https://danrossli.github.io/flowgrow/play/ (the bare address https://danrossli.github.io/flowgrow/ goes there).

**Test build** (the plain look, no graphics, for testing the rules): https://danrossli.github.io/flowgrow/test/

Plain HTML/JS, no build step, no framework, and nothing loaded from other sites. It is a quick prototype: what is left out is listed in CANON.md under "Not built yet".

## How to play

- Tiles arrive in a **pile** that floats over the bottom of the island (LOU-317). The large tile is the one in hand, the next two trail beside it, and a stack of slabs with an "N left" chip shows how many tiles are left. **Drag the tile in hand onto the island** to place it (it lands on the cell it hovers over, and the rings show where it can go), or just tap the land.
- A **habitat** is planted on empty land within reach of your river (the green land), or put on a habitat of the **same level**, which then goes up one level. A different level is never allowed. **Bare ground takes level 1 only** (LOU-300; Dan, Nov 20: "let's implement level 1 only on bare ground"): a higher habitat is planted and lands as level 1, scoring 1 (`bareGroundMode: "downgrade"`, the setting since Dan asked to play test it on Nov 20), or it can only combine onto a habitat of its own level, or be discarded (`"refuse"`). Both settings are in `flowgrow.config.js`.
- There are **15 levels** (Dan, Nov 20: "uncap the tiles max to 15"). The first seven are Sprouts, Meadow, Thicket, Saplings, Grove, Forest and Old forest; the rest are just Level 8 to Level 15 until they are named. A move scores by the level it leaves on the map: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, then 36, 45, 55, 66, 78, 91, 105 and 120.
- There are **three kinds of habitat** to begin with (Dan, Nov 20: "add a yellow tile for "grasslands" and then add a turquoise tile for "marsh""): the original **green** one, **Prairie** (yellow; Dan first said grasslands, then "make yellow like 'prairie'") and **Marsh** (turquoise). All three have the same 15 levels, points and locking, and a habitat is dealt as any of them. A tile only combines with a habitat of its **own kind** and level, and a lock's copies keep the kind of the tile they were copied from. `prairieShare`, `marshShare` and `biomeMatch` in `flowgrow.config.js` set how many are dealt and whether the kind has to match. Each kind has a job (LOU-301). **Marsh must touch the river** (Dan: "turquoise represents marsh and must be adjacent to a river"): it is only planted on empty land that shares an edge with a river tile. **Prairie spreads** (Dan: "Yes, grass can be placed to adjacent grass tiles event if not in the water zone, good idea"): it can also be planted on empty land that shares an edge with another prairie habitat, even outside the river's reach. Green follows the river's reach. `marshNeedsRiver` and `prairieSpreads` switch the two rules. A **fourth kind**, **Trees** (deep green), came with LOU-302 (Dan: "should we have a 'deep green' tile which is trees? And what special should this have?"). Since LOU-314 (Dan, Nov 22: "create a ticket to remove the trees being 'planted over', just make trees a regular tile that needs addition") a tree is an **ordinary habitat**: it is planted as a level 1 tile on empty land within the river's reach, like green (a higher tree follows `bareGroundMode`), it combines **only with a tree of its own level**, and it can't be put on any other habitat, so **no tile ever changes kind**. Its one way past the river's reach is a pine-oneer (the level 4 upgrade, below). A tree that has no legal spot can be discarded like any tile. After the opening (which has no tree), 20% of the dealt habitats are trees (`treeShare`).
- **Upgrades** (LOU-303, built in three parts; Dan: "Make it level 4", "Let's make it an upgrade...and then also should it be an aesthetic difference as well?"): at level 4 (`upgradeLevel`; 0 for none) a marsh, prairie or trees habitat gains an upgrade, a power that also shows in its look. So far (LOU-304) only the marsh has one, the **reed bed**: a marsh habitat of level 4 or higher counts as river bank, so marsh can also be planted on empty land touching it, one tile further inland (it lands as level 1). It is worked out from the tile's kind and level, so nothing is saved for it. A reed bed has a pale rim and three small reeds on it (reeds along its edge in the painted look); tap one that can't take your tile and the toast says what it does. The prairie's and the trees' follow below (LOU-305, LOU-306).
- **The pick** (LOU-305; Dan: "At level 4 one of the upgrades can be \"spreader\"...", "call the ability steppe", "make it \"meadow\" and it adds the score of neighboring marsh and trees as a multiplier"): when a **prairie** habitat reaches level 4 a card asks you to choose **Steppe** (each time it levels up, a free level 1 prairie is planted beside it, `steppePerLevel` of them) or **Meadow** (each time it levels up, the points are multiplied by 1 + the marsh and trees beside it, up to `meadowMax`). You choose before the next move, the choice is permanent and is a move of its own, undo takes it back with the combine that led to it, and a lock's copy keeps it. The steppe has a red-orange rim and a seed-head fan, the meadow a magenta rim and a flower.
- **Trees' roots and pine-oneer** (LOU-306; Dan: "I like roots...put no cap on it, allow chain reaction for combos", "call it pine-oneer....like for pine trees"): a **tree** habitat that reaches level 4 also asks you to choose, **Roots** or **Pine-oneer**. Each time a roots tree levels up, **every lower habitat beside it rises a level for free** and scores like a combine (`rootsPerLevel` limits how many), and a tile that rises counts as levelling up too, so a lifted steppe plants its tile, a lifted meadow multiplies the rise and a lifted roots tree lifts its own neighbours (`rootsChain`): a chain that always ends, since a tile only rises while it is lower than the tree lifting it. **Trees can be planted on empty land next to a pine-oneer**, as level 1, even outside the river's reach. Roots has a tan rim and curling roots, pine-oneer a mint rim and a small pine.
- **Marsh river multiplier** (LOU-309; Dan, Nov 21: "let's do a marsh bonus that for each "additional" river tile, it's a score multiple. 1 river = x1 (normal), 2 river = 2x...3x, 4x, 5x...."): **on** (`marshRiverMultiplier`; it was off until Dan had seen the numbers, then he said "Turn it on"). Every score a marsh tile earns (planted, combined onto, levelled, lifted by Roots) is multiplied by a number that grows with the river tiles that share an edge with it: **×1 for one (or none), ×1.5 for two, ×2 for three, ×2.5 for four, ×3 for five and ×3.5 for six** (`marshRiverRamp`; Dan, Nov 21: "make the marsh multiplier 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5 instead". It was the count itself, ×1 to ×6, and `?marshRiverRamp=1,2,3,4,5,6` plays that ramp). A score can now end in .5. The source and locked river tiles count, and the river is read at the moment of the score. A marsh tile that scores more shows its multiplier ("×2.5"), the land it would score more on shows it too, and the toast says "Marsh ×2.5: +15.". To play without it, add `?marshRiverMultiplier=false` to the address (any config value can be set that way for one visit, as `key=value`; a run and its best score are kept apart per ruleset).
- **Elevation** (LOU-310; Dan, Nov 21: "create a ticket for "elevation" such that there is a "step down" on the tiles, this creates a water fall. what we'll do is that river tiles can 'step down" but for the land tiles there is no adjacency bonus on the step down. Rivers can never "go up"."; then "do LOU-310 but make it a separate map, create 2-3 different variations"): three **separate maps** with heights, at `/elevation/foothills/`, `/elevation/ridges/` and `/elevation/terraces/` (a list is at `/elevation/`). **The usual game at `/` is flat and unchanged.** On a map every land cell has a height from 0 to 3, worked out from the run's seed (so every run has new ground; the source is on the highest ground, mountains are at the top, and neighbouring land differs by at most 1). **A river tile can't be put on land higher than the river tile behind it**: it can go level or down a step, never up, and a river tile that steps down makes a **waterfall**, drawn as white lines. Across a step, Meadow's multiplier doesn't count marsh and trees (`stepAdjacency`: `all` also stops Roots, Steppe, lock copies, prairie spread, reed beds and pine-oneers, `none` stops nothing). The land is shaded by height and a dark line runs along every step. The three shapes (`elevationShape`) are **foothills** (rings falling away from the source, with bumps), **ridges** (ridges run out from the source, lower land between) and **terraces** (broad flat tiers falling away in one direction). Each map keeps its own saved run and best score. `elevation`, `elevationLevels`, `elevationShape` and `stepAdjacency` are in `flowgrow.config.js` (elevation is off there), and `?elevation=true&elevationShape=ridges` plays a map on the usual page for one visit. `node tools/make-maps.mjs` writes the map pages from `index.html` (and `--check` says if they are up to date), and `node tools/flowgrow-sim.mjs --elevation 1000` reports on the land: how much of it a river can reach without going up, and whether the opening can always be played.
- A **river tile** put on empty land that touches the river makes the river longer (that scores nothing). Put it on a river tile of its **own level** and it raises that tile a level, like a habitat, for the points of the new level (**rivers level up**, Dan, Nov 20: "make rivers also level up, just as points make the "width" max 2"). A river tile can reach level 15, but **the width stops at 2**: level 1 reaches 1 tile out and every level from 2 up reaches 2. `riverMaxLevel` and `riverScore` in `flowgrow.config.js` set the top level and what scores (`2` and `"none"` are the first build's rivers). **Rivers rise downstream** (Dan, Nov 20): a river tile can't be lower than the river tile it flows from, so a river is widened from its end back towards the source. Raise the source first and no later level 1 river tile can go anywhere, which would strand the other three river tiles the pile opens with, so **a river tile can't raise a river tile if that would leave another river tile waiting in your pile with no legal move** (`riverRaiseGuard`, Dan, Nov 21: "fix the opening trap"): extend the river first, or discard the others if you do want the raise.
- **Discard** (the round button beside the tile in hand, or drop the tile on it) throws the tile in hand away. **Undo** (top right) steps back as far as you like. A run has 25 tiles (LOU-318; it was 20), with no timer and no way to lose.
- **The pilot pile** (Dan, Nov 20): you start with just **20 tiles** (10 until Dan asked to "expand starting tiles to 20" the same day), **all level 1**, and nothing higher is ever dealt. A tile only goes on a habitat of its own level, so the pile alone can take a habitat to level 2 and no further. Level 3 and up come only from the copies a lock banks, so the loop (place, combine, lock, place the copies) is the challenge. The first eight tiles are four river tiles (Dan, Nov 21: "have it start with 4 water tiles, that way I can build my stream") and then green, green, prairie, marsh. After that a tile is a river tile one time in ten, and a habitat is prairie three times in ten, marsh three times in ten and green otherwise. `stackSize: 80`, `levelOdds: 0.5` and `lockEvery: 10` in `flowgrow.config.js` bring back the first build's game.
- **Locking** (LOU-299): there is **no limit on locks** (Dan, Nov 20): lock as many tiles as you like, as often as you like. **Press and hold** a tile on the island (or right-click it) and confirm (LOU-317: there is no Lock button). Each neighbour at that tile's level or below is copied to the back of your pile, and the locked tile can't grow any more. The run ends when the pile is empty and no lock would bank a copy, or when you tap **End run**.
- Every number is in `flowgrow.config.js`. On the test build add `?seed=123` to the address to replay the same pile (the play page takes `?seed=` only with `?dev=1`).

## Three looks

- **Simple** (the test build is this look and no other, for piloting): flat hexes seen from above. **Blue** tiles are the river and **green**, **yellow** (prairie), **turquoise** (marsh) and **deep green** (trees) tiles are habitats. Every kind gets darker with each level, with a gold rim from level 8 and a second one from level 12. Every one carries its level as a **number**. Empty land the river reaches is pale green, land it doesn't reach is off-white, mountains are grey, and the source has a white ring. A locked tile carries a padlock. Every cell the tile in hand can go to has a dashed ring (LOU-317) with its points inside: gold for a tile you can combine with, teal for a river tile you can raise a level, blue for land the river can extend onto and green for land you can plant on.
- **Painted:** the first look, a hand-painted tilted island with seven kinds of habitat.
- **3D** (LOU-311): a small living island seen from a fixed angle, with a day and night. See "The 3D look" below.

The rules, the pile, locking and the saved run are the same in all three. **The play page opens on the 3D look** and drops quietly to the painted look, and then to the plain one, if the device can't keep up; a player has no look switch (LOU-316). A developer adds `?dev=1&look=simple`, `?dev=1&look=painted` or `?dev=1&look=3d` to the play page for one visit. The test build is always the plain look.

## The 3D look

`?look=3d` (Dan, Nov 22: "this looks good, can you provide very detailed instructions in linear to make it for flowgrow. some changes, you do not need the rotation. include day/night cycles."; LOU-311) draws the same game as a small living island seen from a fixed angle: terrarium tiles with soil layers on the island's edge, ground that blends from tile to tile, plants that spill across tile borders, a green pond, one small animal on each tile, and a day and night. **It changes no rule**: it reads the game and calls `tap()`, and the same seed and moves score the same in every look. **Nothing rotates**: the camera angle never changes; you pan with one finger and zoom with a pinch, the wheel or the zoom button. The whole island fits at the opening view. Nothing man-made is drawn, even at night. When you aim at a tile (the mouse over it, or a finger on it) tall plants in front of it fade so you can see it.

- **The day** runs on a 4-minute clock (`dayLength`: dawn, day, golden hour, dusk, night, pre-dawn) and starts in the morning. It is only light: there is no clock or warning on screen, and it never touches a rule, a score or a tap. The Menu's **Time of day** row (3D look only) cycles Auto, Day, Golden hour, Night and Paused. `?time=day`, `?time=gold` and `?time=night` (also `dawn` and `dusk`, or a number from 0 to 1) hold a moment, and `?time=progress` makes the pile the day (morning at the first tile, night when the pile is empty). Reduced motion and the low tier start in day and hold. At night the sea has a column of moonlight and mirrored stars, fireflies come out, some plants glow a little, and the night animals replace the day ones.
- **An example island** (Dan, Nov 22: "give me an example map where everything is very filled out and the ecosystem is rich"): https://danrossli.github.io/flowgrow/?look=3d&example=rich draws a designed island that is full of every kind, level and upgrade, with the river in three branches. It is only for looking at (pan, zoom, the Time of day row): the game's state, saves and score are not touched and a tap does nothing but say so.
- **Level numbers** (LOU-313; Dan, Nov 22: "have a way such that the level numbers 'blend in' rather than stick out with the pill"): a tile's level is a pale cream numeral pressed into the ground at the front of the tile, with a darker olive shadow, in place of the white pill. A river tile shows its level from 2 (as the pill did). It is part of the ground: it is lit and shaded by the scene (it takes the sun's shadow and lights itself a little at night), it springs in with a tile that has just grown, and it brightens a little on the tile you are aiming at. **It grows when you zoom out** (Dan: "Grow when zoomed out"): about twice its size with the whole island in view, true size zoomed in. Plants, stones and grass keep off a patch of 0.36 round it. All the numbers are one mesh and one material (an atlas of the numbers 1 to 32), so they cost one draw call and about 24 triangles a tile. A locked tile has a small round lock badge where the pill was (Dan: "Small lock badge"), and the number stays. Because the numbers are part of the picture, a list nobody sees (`#l3sr`) tells a screen reader about each tile ("Prairie (level 4), column 3, row 5"). Where it lies and how large it is are in `flowgrow.config.js` under `look3d.numeral`.
- **Quality:** `?q=low|med|high`; without it a short frame-time probe picks one when the look starts. `low` has no shadows and no animals, `med` has one shadow map and one animal on each tile, `high` is the full look. While the whole island is in view the plants and grass are drawn thinner, and a late game that would go over the triangle budget has them thinned further (never below 30 per cent).
- **Files:** `flowgrow.look3d.js` (the camera, ground, sea, river, rings, animals and the work to stay in budget), `flowgrow.look3d.art.js` (what grows on a tile, and the animals) and `flowgrow.look3d.sky.js` (the day and night), with three.js r128 in `vendor/three.r128.min.js` (MIT). They are loaded only for this look. Every number is in `flowgrow.config.js` under `look3d`.
- **Numbers** at 390×844, measured in headless Chrome with software GL on a fresh island, a typical 20-tile run and a heavy late game (80 tiles of mixed kinds, levels 1 to 8), whole island in view. A tile with a shadow is drawn twice, so the shadow pass is listed with the main pass (the page's own stats don't count it). The budget is 250k triangles and 150 draw calls on `med`, and counts both passes.

  | Tier | Fresh island | A 20-tile run | Heavy late game |
  | -- | -- | -- | -- |
  | `low` | 30k triangles, 121 draw calls | 31k, 122 | 58k, 122 |
  | `med` | 52k + 6k shadows, 123 draw calls (+9) | 53k + 10k, 124 (+19) | 98k + 74k, 124 (+98) |
  | `high` | 82k + 11k shadows, 123 (+9) | 86k + 20k, 124 (+19) | 235k + 192k, 124 (+98) |

  Zoomed in (about 4 tiles across) the heavy late game is 52k on `low`, 112k + 96k to 113k + 116k on `med` (it depends on where you look) and 301k + 298k on `high` (thinned to 91 per cent). The frame rates under software GL, which is a processor doing a graphics card's work, are far below a phone's (`low` 27 and 18, `med` 18 and 10, `high` 13 and 6, on the fresh island and the heavy game), and the JavaScript's own time for a frame is about 1 ms on `low` and 3 to 4 ms on `med`. **None of it has been measured on a phone.**
- **Tests:** `node --experimental-websocket tools/look3d-smoke.mjs` (see "Check it"). `window.__flowgrow3d` has `setTime(mode or t)`, `getTime()`, `setQuality(q)`, `setThrottle(on)`, `stats()` (triangles, draw calls, fps, the shadow pass, the level of detail) and `sample()` for scripts. The level numbers are the mesh with `renderOrder` 2.5 in `__flowgrow3d.scene`.
- What was changed from the build spec, cut, or left as a placeholder (the art for levels 8 to 15 and for the four kinds is a proposal) is listed in CANON.md under "The 3D look, no rules changed".

## The game screen

LOU-317 (Dan, Nov 22: "redesign the UI to be better, tidy up the whole screen", "make the tiles coming on 'floating'"). **No panels:** the island fills the screen, and over it are one slim top line (the score, big, with **Undo** and **Menu** at the right), the **pile** floating over the bottom, and **Discard** beside the tile in hand. The bottom is about 214 px at 390×844 (it was about 400).

- **The pile:** the tile in hand (large, with its name above it), the next two trailing right, and a stack of thin slabs, **one for every tile left**, under a small "N left" chip, the only count of the tiles left. The tiles bob slowly and glide forward when one is played; a slab lifts off the stack. Reduced motion: no bobbing or gliding.
- **Placing:** drag the tile in hand onto the island (it hovers over its target with its shadow and a tag with the points, and lands when let go; let go on Discard to discard it; anywhere else and it goes back), or tap land. The rings show on every cell the tile can go to, with the points inside.
- **Locking:** there is no Lock button. **Press and hold a tile on the island, or right-click it**, and a card asks you to confirm. The menu says how, and a one-time tip after the fifth move.
- **The menu** (top right) has Whole island (the old corner zoom button), the levels, the copies waiting at the back of the pile, the run log and a new run.
- `node --experimental-websocket tools/screen-smoke.mjs` plays it with real touches (see "Check it").

## The river's seven stages

A river tile is level 1 to 15 and **looks its level** (LOU-313; Dan, Nov 22: "adjust the design of the river and show progression of the river as it levels up"): wider, deeper and calmer as it rises, with the banks and the wildlife filling in. Levels 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, 6 to 8, 9 to 14, and 15 have the stages 1 to 7 (Trickle, Brook, Stream, Creek, River, Broad river, Great river: placeholders). It is only how a tile looks; no rule reads it. The stage table and the level-to-stage mapping are data in `flowgrow.config.js` (`look3d.riverStages`, `look3d.riverStageOf`, `look3d.riverColor`), and `riverStage(level)` in `flowgrow.js` reads the mapping.

- **In the pile's holder:** seven pictures, `art/pile/river-1.webp` to `river-7.webp`, with the level drawn on them (a stage covers several levels). `node --experimental-websocket tools/bake-pile-sprites.mjs --set river` bakes them from `tools/river-tiles.html`.
- **On the 3D board:** the real river, built from its path across the tiles: the water's width, colour, speed and foam blend smoothly from each tile's stage to the next, a source starts the water, the end of the river is a small round pool and a tile with three river neighbours has a round basin. Along it grow tufts, flowers, reeds and lily pads, willows and birch at the back, ferns, sandbars and a heron; ducks swim on it and a gull circles over the great river.
- `node --experimental-websocket tools/holder-smoke.mjs` and `tools/look3d-smoke.mjs` check it (see "Check it").

## Green is the starter (the takeover, off)

With `greenTakeover` on (LOU-307; Dan, Nov 21: "scrub right now is "any tile" therefore it's special is that any tile can go on top and "take over"") a marsh, prairie or trees habitat put on an unlocked green habitat of its own level turns it into its own kind one level up and scores like a combine, if the green tile is a place the taker could be planted (marsh beside the river or a reed bed, prairie within reach or beside prairie, trees within reach or beside a pine-oneer). **It is off in `flowgrow.config.js`** until Dan has seen the numbers (CANON.md has them, and the rule as built); `greenTakeoverMaxLevel` (15) limits how high a green can be taken over. Try it on the test build: https://danrossli.github.io/flowgrow/test/?greenTakeover=true. A tile in hand that can take over green shows a violet ring, and the toast says "Prairie took over green, level 3." `node tools/flowgrow-sim.mjs 1000 greenTakeover=true` reports what it did (`--green` prints its green-on-the-map line with it off too, to compare), and the search and analyze tools know the move.

## The pile's pictures

The tile holder (the pile floating over the bottom of the island, in every look) draws an illustrated tile for the level 1 tiles the pile deals (green, prairie, marsh and trees) and for the river's seven stages (see "The river's seven stages"; LOU-312; Dan, Nov 22: "write a ticket for these tiles in the tile holder"). They are five still pictures in `art/pile/` (`green.webp`, `prairie.webp`, `marsh.webp`, `trees.webp` and, for the river, `river-1.webp` to `river-7.webp`: 256×256 WebP with a transparent background, each 40 KB or less and 250 KB or less in all), baked once from the Flowgrow Pile Tiles page, so the holder never loads three.js. Each has the level number (1) pressed into the ground at the front of the tile, in the way the 3D look draws it (LOU-313), so a picture carries no pill and the tile's accessible label says its level ("Marsh (level 1)"). The tile in hand is about 150 px across (a 172 px box), the next two 57 and 43 px, and the copies at the back of the pile, which the menu lists, 34 px; they bob slowly unless the player asks for less motion (LOU-317).

Only a tile that is level 1 with no upgrade has a picture, and a river tile of any level (its stage's picture, with its level drawn on it). A copy from a lock can be any level, an upgraded tile or a raised river tile, and those keep the flat tile with its number (`// placeholder: art for levels 2+ is not made yet`). A picture that is missing or fails to load is simply not used: its tile is drawn flat, so the holder is never empty. To bake the pictures again: `node --experimental-websocket tools/bake-pile-sprites.mjs` (the habitats) or `node --experimental-websocket tools/bake-pile-sprites.mjs --set river` (the river's seven stages; a few seconds, and the same bytes each time). It serves this folder, opens `tools/pile-tiles.html?bake=1` in headless Chrome (a copy of the reference page with three.js from `vendor/`, one fixed camera at azimuth 0.5 and polar 0.9, no sea, sky or labels), crops the five with one common scale and writes the files; it fails if a file is over 40 KB or the five are over 250 KB. `--numeral N` bakes another number (0 for none), and `--out DIR` writes them elsewhere.

## The play page

`/play/` (https://danrossli.github.io/flowgrow/play/; LOU-316, Dan, Nov 22: "the second is to have an endpoint for people to play which will have the graphics this will be canon and used throughout the game") is the public game, with the graphics. **Play page = canon look. Test build = canon rules, no art.** If the two ever disagree on a rule, the rules file wins and it is a bug. The bare address redirects here with its query string and hash kept (a link that carries rules or a seed goes to `/test/` instead).

- **One game for a player:** it opens on the 3D look (with the pile's pictures and the level numbers on the ground), with no look row in the menu and no rules, seed or look in the address. It loads the shared `flowgrow.js` and `flowgrow.config.js` and carries no rule of its own.
- **Quiet fallback:** if WebGL is missing, or the first frames run under 20 fps, it drops to the painted look, and from there to the plain look, in the same run (a look only reads the game). Nothing is said and nothing is remembered. Reduced motion shows a still picture, and so does low battery (no shadows or animals, the day held).
- **First run:** three short screens (place a tile, match two of the same level, grow the river), once; skipping or finishing is remembered.
- **Share-ready:** title, description, icon, theme colour and a 1200×630 preview image (`share.png`, a 3D screenshot of a grown island), in the page and in the bare address's redirect page.
- **Saved runs:** one per device under `flowgrow.run.v13.<ruleset>` (the test build's are `flowgrow.test.*`, a developer's `flowgrow.dev.*`). A run saved by older rules is dropped with a plain line.
- **Developer's door:** `?dev=1` turns on `?look=` and `?seed=` (not the rules: they stay on the test build), shows a small dev line and keeps its own saved run; with `?dev=1&fps=N` the fallback probe sees N frames a second, to try the fallback.
- **Hooks for the rest of the game, empty for now:** `mode` in `flowgrow.config.js` ("free"; later "daily" and "challenge"), and `Flowgrow.onRunEnd(result)`, called when a run ends with `{flowgrow, v, rules, seed, config, moves, score, top, mode}`, the same record as the test build's Copy run. A small version shows in the menu only.
- `node --experimental-websocket tools/play-page-smoke.mjs` checks all of it (see "Check it").

## The test build

`/test/` (https://danrossli.github.io/flowgrow/test/; LOU-315, Dan, Nov 22: "one is to keep the game without the graphics...this will make it easier to test and iterate") is Flowgrow with **no graphics**: the plain look only, on its own page, with no art, no 3D and no sound. It shares the rules (`flowgrow.js`) and the config (`flowgrow.config.js`) with the game and has none of its own. **It is the place for rules work, and it never gets graphics.** Any config value can be changed in the address (`?seed=123`, `?treeShare=0`, `?bareGroundMode=refuse`, `?upgradeLevel=0` ...; unknown names are ignored) and a rules line at the bottom says which rules the address changed. Its menu has **Copy run** (the rules, seed and moves as one line of text), **Load run** (paste one to replay it; under other rules the page opens with them) and **Reload** (keeps the run). The board is also data: every cell is an element with `data-kind`, `data-level` and `data-cell` (`#boardDom`, `#pileDom`), so a script can read it without pixels. It keeps its own saved run (`flowgrow.test.*`) and has its own service worker (`test/sw.js`, cache `fgtest-`). `node --experimental-websocket tools/test-page-smoke.mjs` checks it, and fails if an image, font, three.js or other art request ever appears.

## Check it

- `node tools/flowgrow-sim.mjs --test` runs the rule checks. All must pass. They use fixed first-build values (80 tiles, level odds 0.5), so tuning `flowgrow.config.js` never breaks them.
- `node tools/flowgrow-sim.mjs` plays 1,000 runs of the game in `flowgrow.config.js` (the pilot pile with unlimited locks now; add `stackSize=80 levelOdds=0.5 lockEvery=10` for the first build's game), with the game's own rules. ## Search it

Three developer tools (plain Node, no packages) find out how high a set of rules lets scores go, so a rule idea can be tested before it is built into the game:

- `node tools/flowgrow-search.mjs --workers 8 --seconds 300` looks for the highest score on one seed with the game's own rules. Any config value can be changed on the command line as `key=value`, to try a rule without editing the config, for example `node tools/flowgrow-search.mjs maxLevel=7 riverMonotone=false`. It saves the best run to `search-best.json`. `--picks steppe,pine` fixes the upgrades it picks (otherwise the policy search tunes the choice and nrpa chooses as it goes).
- `node tools/flowgrow-analyze.mjs search-best.json` replays a saved run and says where its points came from (planted or combined, copies or dealt tiles), which tiles were locked and what the island looks like at the end.
- `node --experimental-websocket tools/flowgrow-replay.mjs search-best.json` plays a saved run in the real page (it needs Chrome) and checks that the page scores the same. The page plays by its own rules, so it only accepts runs found under them.

To test a rule: give it a config switch, search with it on and with it off, and compare the best score, where the points come from, and how many locks and moves it takes. The default seed, 495454692, is the run Dan pasted on Nov 20 (47 by hand).

## Deploy

GitHub Pages, by the workflow in `.github/workflows/pages.yml`, on every push to `main`. A push isn't live until that run succeeds: check `gh run list --limit 3`, then fetch a live file that the commit changed. If the run fails within seconds with "No artifacts named github-pages were found", `gh run rerun <run id>` fixes it (a glitch on GitHub's side); if the rerun fails the same way, `gh workflow run "Deploy to GitHub Pages"` starts a fresh run. Bump `VERSION` in `sw.js` whenever a cached file changes, and add new files to its `LOCAL` list.

## Saved data

Flowgrow shares its address (`danrossli.github.io`) with Flow Tile, so everything it keeps is named for it: browser storage keys start with `flowgrow.` (the best score, kept per ruleset, and the run in progress) and its cache is `flowgrow-vN`. It never reads or clears anything of Flow Tile's, apart from carrying a best score and a run in progress saved before the move (`flowtile.flowgrow.`) across once.
