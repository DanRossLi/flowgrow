### No lock limit, as built (Nov 20)

- **What changed:** `lockEvery` 10 to 0 in `flowgrow.config.js`. A lock is always at hand, the Lock button shows ∞ and no "next in", and `lockHold` is not used. The first build's pacing is one value away: `lockEvery: 10` with `lockHold: 1`.
- **What still limits the loop:** a tile can be locked once, and a locked tile can't be copied, combined onto, widened or locked again, so the loop runs out when no unlocked tile has a copyable neighbour. The run then ends by itself, or when you tap **End run**.
- **What it does:** `node tools/flowgrow-sim.mjs` reported about 47 points for its simple player (before the river rule) over about 300 moves and 77 locks. That player locks whichever tile banks the most copies, river tiles included, so the river spreads over most of the island and scores nothing. A player who locks only habitats stops by itself after 2 to 3 locks and about 20 moves, with 47 to 57 points (best 64), against 29 to 35 with one lock.

### The river rule, as built (Nov 20)

- **The rule:** a river tile can't be lower than the river tile behind it, the one it flows from (the one it is drawn joined to). So a new river tile must be at least as high as the tile it joins, and a tile can only be widened once every tile that joins it is already that high. Equal is allowed, and each branch is checked against its own tile. River copies from locks follow the same rule.
- **What it means in play:** a river is widened from its end back towards the source, never the other way round. If the source is widened first, every later level 1 river tile has no move at all (nothing can follow a level 2 tile at level 1, and no level 1 tile is left to widen), so they are only good for the discard button.
- **Where it is:** `riverMonotone` in `flowgrow.config.js` (`false` is the first build's rule). In the rules part of `flowgrow.js`: `options()` decides what can go where and `why()` gives the reason when a tile can't. The simulator's rule checks stay on the first build's values (`riverMonotone: false`), and 8 new checks cover the rule.

### The search tools, as built (Nov 20)

- **What is there:** `tools/flowgrow-search.mjs` looks for the highest score on one seed, with a hand-built "sweep" player tuned by hill climbing and a nested rollout search (`--workers N` runs N processes at once). `tools/flowgrow-analyze.mjs` replays a saved run and says where its points came from. `tools/flowgrow-replay.mjs` plays a saved run in the real page and checks the score. They are developer tools: nothing in the game loads them. Plain Node, no packages. The replay needs Chrome and Node's built-in WebSocket (`--experimental-websocket` on Node 20).
- **Testing a rule:** any config value can be changed on the command line as `key=value`, so a rule that has a config switch can be searched with it on and off without editing the config. A saved run remembers the changes it was found with.
- **What they found** (seed 495454692, Nov 20): 47 by hand, 918 before the river rule and 977 after it, with the top level at 7. The default seed is that run. The findings and two balance questions for Dan are in the Linear document "Flowgrow balance brainstorm", attached to LOU-299.

### The bare ground rule, as built (Nov 20, LOU-300)

- **The rule:** a habitat can be planted on empty land as it is only up to `bareGroundMaxLevel`, which is 1. What happens to a habitat above that depends on `bareGroundMode`, built both ways so they can be compared:
  - **`"refuse"`** (~~the default until the numbers are in; Dan may flip it after the comparison~~ *Dan flipped it on Nov 20: `"downgrade"` is the setting now*): the move is not offered. The tile can only **combine** onto a habitat of its own level, or be **discarded**. Tapping bare ground with it says "Only level 1 can be planted on bare ground. Combine it or discard it." and the island shows no plant outlines for it.
  - **`"downgrade"`**: the tile can be planted, and it **lands as a level 1 habitat** of the same kind (there is one kind, habitat). It scores as level 1 (+1). The caption over the pile says "… · plants as level 1" (so a copy reads "Thicket (level 3) copy · plants as level 1"), every plantable tile carries an "as 1" tag, the toast after planting says "Planted as level 1.", the result counts them ("2 planted (2 as level 1)") and the run log says where one landed as level 1.
- **The setting (Nov 20):** `bareGroundMode` is `"downgrade"` in the config, because Dan asked to play test it ("put downgrade in the game and let me play test"). Because the mode is part of the ruleset key, a run in progress under `"refuse"` starts fresh when the page loads, and best scores are kept apart per mode.
- **Unchanged:** combining and scoring (the only new case is the downgrade landing as level 1). **Copies stay exact:** Dan's rule 7, "it's not the reduced level", is about what a lock copies, not about planting. A level 4 copy is a level 4 tile in the pile, and only planting it on bare ground lowers it, and only in `"downgrade"`. Copies now act as fuel for climbing: a level 4 copy needs a level 4 habitat on the map to become level 5.
- **Habitats only.** River tiles keep today's rules (extend onto empty land, widen, rivers rise downstream), and **river widening is not changed here**: Dan wants to see this rule's effect first, because level 2 river lockers matter less once copies can't be dumped on bare ground.
- **Lock capping: none** (LOU-300: "get rid of lock capping"): no lock limit, pacing or cost was added, and `lockEvery` stays 0. An earlier idea to require a grown neighbour pair before locking was dropped.
- **Where it is:** `bareGroundMaxLevel` and `bareGroundMode` in `flowgrow.config.js`, both part of the ruleset key so best scores are kept per ruleset. In the rules part of `flowgrow.js`, `plantLevel()` gives the level a habitat lands as on empty land (0: refused), `options()` uses it, `why()` explains a refusal and `place()` puts the tile on at that level. With `bareGroundMaxLevel` at 15 (the top level) the old rule is back in either mode, and the simulator prints exactly what it printed before (checked: 200 runs of the default config and 1,000 runs of the first build's, both modes, byte for byte).
- **Saved runs:** the save key is now `flowgrow.run.v3`, and a save also carries its ruleset key, so a save from older rules starts a fresh run instead of replaying moves that may no longer be legal. The dead `run.v2` is removed when the page loads.
- **Also changed:** the menu's how-to says the rule in force, and its lock line no longer says "Every 10 tiles you place earns a lock" (a leftover from before Dan's no-limit rule). The search tool's hand-built player now combines a tile it can't plant and plants one it can't combine (it used to discard).
- **Checked:** the rule checks cover both modes (levels 1, 2 and 3 on bare ground, combining, a level 2 river tile, a copy that can't be placed being discarded and the run still ending, a whole run of random moves replaying from its seed, undo over a downgraded tile); the page was played at 390×844 in both modes, with undo and a reload with a downgraded tile on the map.
- **What the search found** (seed 495454692, Nov 20; one 5-minute search per setting, 10 processes each, run at the same time, so search results and not proofs):
  - **Rule off** (`bareGroundMaxLevel` 15, the control): **1283**, top level 7, 42 locks, 194 moves. Points from copies planted on bare ground 480 (37%), copies combined 787 (61%), dealt tiles 16 (1%).

The rule took the best score found down by about two thirds and moved the points from planting copies to combining them, without stopping the loop (34 and 41 locks). The two settings are close, and the search can't yet separate them: the refuse search was still improving when it stopped (361 at 16 seconds, 441 at 248) while the downgrade one stood at 471 from 14 seconds. ### Prairie and marsh, as built (Nov 20)

- **What changed:** two new kinds of habitat tile, **prairie** (yellow, first called grasslands) and **marsh** (turquoise), next to the original **green** one, and a pile of **20** tiles (`stackSize` 20, up from 10). They are habitat tiles in every other way: levels 1 to 15, the same points, planted on empty land within reach of the river, discarded, locked and copied like the green ones, and dealt at level 1 only like every dealt tile.
- **Kinds and combining** (my reading, listed under Open for Dan): a tile only combines with a habitat of its **own kind** and level (`biomeMatch: true`; `false` lets any kind combine, and the habitat then keeps its own kind). A lock's copies keep the kind of the neighbour they are copied from (copies stay exact), and a tile planted by "downgrade" keeps its kind too. Tapping a habitat of another kind says "That's Marsh (level 1). Only a tile of the same kind and level goes on it."
- **The pile:** the first five tiles are always green 1, green 1, river, prairie 1, marsh 1, so every kind shows on the first screen. After that a tile is a river tile one time in ten, and a dealt habitat is prairie 3 times in 10 (`prairieShare` 0.3), marsh 3 times in 10 (`marshShare` 0.3) and green otherwise. The two shares can't pass 1 together. With both at 0 a run is dealt exactly as it was before the kinds existed.
- **Looks:** in the simple look prairie is yellow (vivid at level 1, deepening to dark amber) and marsh is turquoise (bright at level 1, deepening to dark teal), each darker with every level like the green ones, with the number on them as before. - **Names:** "Marsh" is Dan's word, and so is "Prairie" (he first said grasslands, then "make yellow like 'prairie'"). The original green habitat has no name of its own in the game (its levels keep Sprouts to Old forest).
- **Saved runs:** the ruleset key now includes the two shares and the kinds switch, so a run saved before this starts fresh. A run keeps `hb`, the list of which habitats are not green, and it is rebuilt from its seed and moves like everything else.
- **Checked:** 146 rule checks (19 new for kinds). With both shares at 0 the simulator prints exactly what it printed before, byte for byte (1,000 runs of the pilot pile in both bare ground modes, and of the first build's). ### Rivers that level up, as built (Nov 20)

- **What changed:** a river tile can now level up like a habitat, past level 2. A river tile put on a river tile of its **own level** raises that tile a level (the move the code calls "widen"), up to `riverMaxLevel` (15, like habitats), and it scores the points for the level it reaches: 3 for level 2, 6 for level 3, 10 for level 4 and so on, the same table as habitats. Before, a river tile stopped at level 2 and scored nothing.
- **The width stops at 2:** only the first two levels widen the river. A level 1 river tile reaches 1 tile out, and level 2 and every level above it reach 2 (`riverReach` is still `[1, 2]`, and a level past the list reaches as far as the last). The river is drawn no wider than level 2 either. In the simple look a river tile gets a deeper blue with each level and carries its number, like a habitat, with the same gold rims from level 8 and 12.
- **What scores** (`riverScore`; my reading, listed under Open for Dan): `"levels"` is the setting, so only raising a river tile a level scores. Extending the river onto empty land scores nothing, because a high-level river tile put on empty land would be the free planting that the bare ground rule (LOU-300) closed for habitats. `"all"` scores every river move like a habitat of its level, extending too. `"none"` is the first build (rivers score nothing). `riverMaxLevel: 2` with `riverScore: "none"` is exactly the first build's rivers.
- **Rivers still rise downstream** (Dan's rule): a new river tile can't be lower than the tile it joins, and a tile can only be raised once every tile that joins it is already at the new level, so a river is levelled up from its end back towards the source. Locking, copies (a copy keeps its level) and the lock level cap work on river tiles as before.
- **Words in the page:** the ring on a river tile it can raise says "wider +3" while the raise still widens the river, and "+6" after that. A refusal reads "Only a level 4 river tile levels this up.", and at the top level "River level 15 is the top level." The menu has a line about river levels.
- **Saved runs:** the ruleset key includes the two river settings, so a run saved before this starts fresh.
- **Checked:** 160 rule checks (14 new for river levels). With `riverMaxLevel=2 riverScore=none` the simulator prints exactly what it printed before, byte for byte (1,000 runs of the pilot pile in both bare ground modes, and of the first build's). ### Marsh on the bank, prairie that spreads, as built (Nov 20, LOU-301)

- **Marsh must touch the river** (`marshNeedsRiver`, on): marsh can be planted only on empty land that **shares an edge with a river tile**. The source counts and so does a locked river tile. The check is at planting only: combining marsh onto marsh needs nothing new (the target is already on the bank), and a river never shrinks, so a planted marsh tile stays valid. Lock copies of marsh follow the same rule, and in `"downgrade"` a marsh copy still lands as level 1, only on a legal bank tile. Width doesn't matter: marsh uses only the tile next to the river, never the level 2 reach. A marsh tile with no legal bank tile can still combine onto marsh of its own level, or be discarded. The caption over the pile then says "no bank tile free", and tapping a refused tile says "Marsh needs a tile next to the river."
- **Prairie spreads** (`prairieSpreads`, on): prairie can be planted on empty land that **shares an edge with another prairie habitat of any level** (locked or not), **even outside the river's reach**. It can still be planted anywhere inside reach, so the spread only adds tiles, and a prairie tile planted outside reach can spread again. It must be empty land (not a mountain, a river tile or an occupied tile), and a tile planted this way follows the bare ground rule (level 1 only, by `bareGroundMode`). Tapping a refused tile says "Prairie needs a tile within reach of the river or next to prairie."
- **Green keeps its own rule:** reach only. Combining is unchanged (same kind and level). The three kinds now have three jobs: marsh hugs the river, prairie spreads inland, green follows the river's reach.
- **Where it is:** `plantableFor()` in the rules part of `flowgrow.js` gives the land a tile can be planted on, by kind. `options()`, `why()` and the preview use it, so every legal tile is outlined. With both switches off a run is exactly the game before.
- **Saved runs:** the save key is now `flowgrow.run.v4` (the older ones are removed on load), and the ruleset key includes `marshNeedsRiver` and `prairieSpreads`, so older saves start fresh.
- **What the numbers say** (seed 495454692; the hard search is one 5-minute search of 10 processes per setting, run at the same time, so search results and not proofs; the simple player is 1,000 runs):
  - **Rules off** (the game before): best **750**, top level 5, 65 locks, 306 moves. Points: river level-ups 346 (46%), combining copies 320 (43%), copies planted 54 (7%), dealt tiles 30 (4%). 63 habitats and 43 river tiles with the source; 3 land cells empty at the end.
  - **Rules on:** best **1058**, top level 6, 72 locks, 340 moves. Points: combining copies 718 (68%), river level-ups 224 (21%), copies planted 86 (8%), dealt tiles 36 (3%). 95 habitats but only 8 river tiles with the source; 6 land cells empty at the end.
  - **Marsh:** in the best run 36 marsh tiles were in hand and 24 of those turns (67%) had no bank tile free to plant on, and none was discarded (they combined onto marsh).   - **Prairie:** the best run planted 55 of its 164 prairie tiles beyond the river's reach (34%), with 6.9 extra tiles offered per prairie tile in hand. The simple player used the spread 1.8 times a run, with 3.3 extra tiles offered.

### What is built (LOU-298)

- **Words:** a tile is a **habitat** or a **river tile**. Putting a habitat on empty land is **planting**. Two habitats of the same level **combine**. A river tile **extends** the river or **widens** it. The **pile** is the tiles still to come, and the large one is the tile **in hand**.
- **A turn:** tap a tile on the island and the tile in hand goes there. One tap, no confirm. A tile that can't go there gets a one-line message that says why.
- **Screen:** Score, Tiles left and Top level at the top. ~~Menu, the pile (the tile in hand large, the next 3 small), Discard and Undo at the bottom.~~ *Changed Nov 19, see "The pile is easy to find" below.* Land the tile in hand can be planted on has a faint outline. A habitat it can combine with has a ring and its points ("+6"). A river tile it can widen has a ring that says "wider".
- **The seven levels** are painted with the river game's own tree, shrub and flower painters, each bigger and taller than the one before. Every habitat carries a white pill with its level, on the island and in the pile.
- **The land shows reach:** land within reach of the river is grass, and land outside it is bare ground.
- **A run** is its seed and its moves. Undo, saving and reload all rebuild it from those, so a tile is never re-rolled. A finished run is never restored, and a run older than 12 hours starts fresh. Add `?seed=123` to the address to replay the same pile.
- **The rules sit apart from the page:** part 1 of `flowgrow.js` is plain functions that never touch the page, and `tools/flowgrow-sim.mjs` loads that same file in Node.

### Locking, as built (LOU-299)

- **What can be locked:** any unlocked habitat or river tile, the source included (it counts as a level 1 river tile).
- **What a lock copies:** each neighbour that is an unlocked habitat or river tile at or below the locked tile's level. A higher neighbour gives nothing ("it's a yes/no on copies"). Empty land, mountains and locked tiles give nothing. A copy is exact: the neighbour's kind at its full level. The original stays on the map.
- **Where copies go:** to the back of the pile, behind every tile still to be dealt and behind copies banked earlier. They are kept as their own list, and "Tiles left" counts them.
- **A locked tile** can't be combined onto, widened, locked again or copied. It keeps its score and still counts as the top level for dealing.
- **Copies play like dealt tiles,** with the same rules and the same scoring. A level 2 river copy can only extend, and it reaches 2 tiles out.
- **Screen:** a **Lock** button in the bottom bar shows the locks held and the tiles until the next one (since Nov 20 there is no lock limit, so it shows ∞). Tapping it turns lock mode on, and tapping it again turns it off. In lock mode every lockable tile shows how many copies it would bank. Tapping one marks each copy on its neighbour, marks neighbours that are too high, and a card above the bar asks to confirm ("Lock this thicket?"). A locked habitat gets a padlock on its level pill and is painted a little muted. At the back of the pile panel, **Back of the pile** lists the banked copies in order.
- **A lock is a move:** it can be undone, it is saved, and it has a line in the run log. The log marks each placed tile as dealt or copy. The result adds locks used and lost, and copies banked, placed and discarded, with the points from copies.
- **The six lock values** are in `flowgrow.config.js`: `lockEnabled`, `lockLevelCap`, `lockCopiesGo`, `lockRivers`, `lockEvery`, `lockHold`. With `lockEnabled: false` the game plays exactly as it did before locking.
