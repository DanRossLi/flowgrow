### No lock limit, as built (Nov 20)

- **What changed:** `lockEvery` 10 to 0 in `flowgrow.config.js`. A lock is always at hand, the Lock button shows ∞ and no "next in", and `lockHold` is not used. The first build's pacing is one value away: `lockEvery: 10` with `lockHold: 1`.
- **What still limits the loop:** a tile can be locked once, and a locked tile can't be copied, combined onto, widened or locked again, so the loop runs out when no unlocked tile has a copyable neighbour. The run then ends by itself, or when you tap **End run**.
- **What it does:** `node tools/flowgrow-sim.mjs` reported about 47 points for its simple player (before the river rule) over about 300 moves and 77 locks. ### The river rule, as built (Nov 20)

- **The rule:** a river tile can't be lower than the river tile behind it, the one it flows from (the one it is drawn joined to). So a new river tile must be at least as high as the tile it joins, and a tile can only be widened once every tile that joins it is already that high. Equal is allowed, and each branch is checked against its own tile. River copies from locks follow the same rule.
- **What it means in play:** a river is widened from its end back towards the source, never the other way round. If the source is widened first, every later level 1 river tile has no move at all (nothing can follow a level 2 tile at level 1, and no level 1 tile is left to widen), so they are only good for the discard button.
- **Where it is:** `riverMonotone` in `flowgrow.config.js` (`false` is the first build's rule). In the rules part of `flowgrow.js`: `options()` decides what can go where and `why()` gives the reason when a tile can't. The simulator's rule checks stay on the first build's values (`riverMonotone: false`), and 8 new checks cover the rule.

### The search tools, as built (Nov 20)

- **What is there:** `tools/flowgrow-search.mjs` looks for the highest score on one seed, with a hand-built "sweep" player tuned by hill climbing and a nested rollout search (`--workers N` runs N processes at once). `tools/flowgrow-analyze.mjs` replays a saved run and says where its points came from. `tools/flowgrow-replay.mjs` plays a saved run in the real page and checks the score. They are developer tools: nothing in the game loads them. Plain Node, no packages. The replay needs Chrome and Node's built-in WebSocket (`--experimental-websocket` on Node 20).
- **Testing a rule:** any config value can be changed on the command line as `key=value`, so a rule that has a config switch can be searched with it on and off without editing the config. A saved run remembers the changes it was found with.

### The bare ground rule, as built (Nov 20, LOU-300)

- **The rule:** a habitat can be planted on empty land as it is only up to `bareGroundMaxLevel`, which is 1. What happens to a habitat above that depends on `bareGroundMode`, built both ways so they can be compared:
  - **`"refuse"`** (~~the default until the numbers are in; Dan may flip it after the comparison~~ *Dan flipped it on Nov 20: `"downgrade"` is the setting now*): the move is not offered. The tile can only **combine** onto a habitat of its own level, or be **discarded**. Tapping bare ground with it says "Only level 1 can be planted on bare ground. Combine it or discard it." and the island shows no plant outlines for it.
  - **`"downgrade"`**: the tile can be planted, and it **lands as a level 1 habitat** of the same kind (there is one kind, habitat). It scores as level 1 (+1). The caption over the pile says "… · plants as level 1" (so a copy reads "Thicket (level 3) copy · plants as level 1"), every plantable tile carries an "as 1" tag, the toast after planting says "Planted as level 1.", the result counts them ("2 planted (2 as level 1)") and the run log says where one landed as level 1.
- **The setting (Nov 20):** `bareGroundMode` is `"downgrade"` in the config, because Dan asked to play test it ("put downgrade in the game and let me play test"). Because the mode is part of the ruleset key, a run in progress under `"refuse"` starts fresh when the page loads, and best scores are kept apart per mode.
- **Unchanged:** combining and scoring (the only new case is the downgrade landing as level 1). **Copies stay exact:** Dan's rule 7, "it's not the reduced level", is about what a lock copies, not about planting. ### Rivers that level up, as built (Nov 20)

- **What changed:** a river tile can now level up like a habitat, past level 2. A river tile put on a river tile of its **own level** raises that tile a level (the move the code calls "widen"), up to `riverMaxLevel` (15, like habitats), and it scores the points for the level it reaches: 3 for level 2, 6 for level 3, 10 for level 4 and so on, the same table as habitats. Before, a river tile stopped at level 2 and scored nothing.
- **The width stops at 2:** only the first two levels widen the river. A level 1 river tile reaches 1 tile out, and level 2 and every level above it reach 2 (`riverReach` is still `[1, 2]`, and a level past the list reaches as far as the last). The river is drawn no wider than level 2 either. In the simple look a river tile gets a deeper blue with each level and carries its number, like a habitat, with the same gold rims from level 8 and 12.
- **What scores** (`riverScore`; my reading, listed under Open for Dan): `"levels"` is the setting, so only raising a river tile a level scores. Extending the river onto empty land scores nothing, because a high-level river tile put on empty land would be the free planting that the bare ground rule (LOU-300) closed for habitats. `"all"` scores every river move like a habitat of its level, extending too. `"none"` is the first build (rivers score nothing). `riverMaxLevel: 2` with `riverScore: "none"` is exactly the first build's rivers.
- **Rivers still rise downstream** (Dan's rule): a new river tile can't be lower than the tile it joins, and a tile can only be raised once every tile that joins it is already at the new level, so a river is levelled up from its end back towards the source. Locking, copies (a copy keeps its level) and the lock level cap work on river tiles as before.
- **Words in the page:** the ring on a river tile it can raise says "wider +3" while the raise still widens the river, and "+6" after that. A refusal reads "Only a level 4 river tile levels this up.", and at the top level "River level 15 is the top level." The menu has a line about river levels.
- **Saved runs:** the ruleset key includes the two river settings, so a run saved before this starts fresh.
- **Checked:** 160 rule checks (14 new for river levels). With `riverMaxLevel=2 riverScore=none` the simulator prints exactly what it printed before, byte for byte (1,000 runs of the pilot pile in both bare ground modes, and of the first build's). A 25-second search found 637, with 163 of its points from river level-ups, and the page replays it to the same score. ### Marsh on the bank, prairie that spreads, as built (Nov 20, LOU-301)

- **Marsh must touch the river** (`marshNeedsRiver`, on): marsh can be planted only on empty land that **shares an edge with a river tile**. The source counts and so does a locked river tile. The check is at planting only: combining marsh onto marsh needs nothing new (the target is already on the bank), and a river never shrinks, so a planted marsh tile stays valid. Lock copies of marsh follow the same rule, and in `"downgrade"` a marsh copy still lands as level 1, only on a legal bank tile. Width doesn't matter: marsh uses only the tile next to the river, never the level 2 reach. A marsh tile with no legal bank tile can still combine onto marsh of its own level, or be discarded. The caption over the pile then says "no bank tile free", and tapping a refused tile says "Marsh needs a tile next to the river."
- **Prairie spreads** (`prairieSpreads`, on): prairie can be planted on empty land that **shares an edge with another prairie habitat of any level** (locked or not), **even outside the river's reach**. It can still be planted anywhere inside reach, so the spread only adds tiles, and a prairie tile planted outside reach can spread again. It must be empty land (not a mountain, a river tile or an occupied tile), and a tile planted this way follows the bare ground rule (level 1 only, by `bareGroundMode`). Tapping a refused tile says "Prairie needs a tile within reach of the river or next to prairie."
- **Green keeps its own rule:** reach only. Combining is unchanged (same kind and level). The three kinds now have three jobs: marsh hugs the river, prairie spreads inland, green follows the river's reach.
- **Where it is:** `plantableFor()` in the rules part of `flowgrow.js` gives the land a tile can be planted on, by kind. `options()`, `why()` and the preview use it, so every legal tile is outlined. With both switches off a run is exactly the game before.

### Trees that overgrow, as built (Nov 21, LOU-302)

*Superseded Nov 22 by LOU-314 (see "Trees are a regular habitat" below): the overgrow rule was removed. This section is the history of that rule, not a live rule. The numbers in it are from the build of that day, and the `overgrow` key it names no longer exists.*

- **The kind:** **Trees**, deep green, a fourth kind of habitat next to green, prairie and marsh (`b: "t"` on a tile). - **The rule** (`overgrow`, on): a tree is **never planted on bare ground**, in either `bareGroundMode` (a level 1 tree in `"downgrade"` mode too). ### Upgrades part 1: the reed bed and the look system, as built (Nov 21, LOU-304)

- **The upgrade level** (`upgradeLevel`, 4): the level at which a marsh, prairie or trees habitat gains an upgrade. 0 means no upgrades. Below it nothing changes. Only the marsh's upgrade is built so far; prairie and trees gain nothing yet, and neither does green (it has no upgrade).
- **The reed bed** (marsh): a marsh habitat of level 4 or higher is a **reed bed**. Marsh can then be planted on empty land that **shares an edge with it**, as if it were river bank, which opens ground one tile further inland. So LOU-301's rule becomes: marsh must touch a river tile **or a reed bed**. It is **derived from the tile's kind and level and is not stored**: no new state and no new move, so it replays, saves and undoes with the level that made it.
- **What it does and doesn't do:** a marsh tile planted this way lands as **level 1**, by the bare ground rule (LOU-300): under `"refuse"` only a level 1 marsh tile can be planted there, and under `"downgrade"` a higher one lands as level 1. A marsh tile planted beside a reed bed is only level 1, so it is not a reed bed itself. The upgrade is **lost when the tile stops being marsh**: a level 4 tree that overgrows a reed bed makes a level 5 tree, and marsh no longer plants beside it (marsh planted beside it earlier stays). A lock copy of a reed bed is still level 4 marsh, so it still counts: combined onto a level 4 marsh habitat it makes a level 5 reed bed. A **locked reed bed still counts**, as a locked river tile does (my reading, below). No protection upgrade exists (Dan: "it's not a combat game").
- **Words in the page:** a refused marsh tile says "Marsh needs a tile next to the river or a reed bed." (with `upgradeLevel: 0` it says the old "Marsh needs a tile next to the river."), the preview outlines every legal tile, and the pile's caption still says "no bank tile free" when there is none. The menu says marsh goes "on land that touches the river or a reed bed" and has a line on the reed bed.
- **The look system** (Dan: "should it be an aesthetic difference as well?"): one table in the page, `UPG_LOOK`, gives each upgrade its look, so the next four (steppe, meadow, roots, pine-oneer) plug in with their own entry and an id in `UPGRADE` in the rules, and nothing is reworked. A copy in the pile has it too.
- **Tapping a tile:** a tap places the tile in hand, so the one plain line shows in two places: when a tap can't place the tile in hand on a tile that has an upgrade, the toast adds "Reed bed: marsh can grow one tile further inland." after the reason, and when a move gives a tile its upgrade (a combine to level 4) the toast is that line. A tile's tooltip in the pile names its upgrade.
- **Where it is:** `upgradeLevel` in `flowgrow.config.js`, part of the ruleset key. ### Upgrades part 2: the pick, steppe and meadow, as built (Nov 21, LOU-305)

- **The pick:** when a **prairie** habitat first reaches the upgrade level (4) with no upgrade, by a combine (or planted at that level, which the default bare ground rule doesn't allow), the player **picks Steppe or Meadow**. The choice is permanent. It is stored on the tile (`S.hf`, `{"x,y": id}`) and is **a move of its own** (`{a:"u", x, y, f}`, a line in the run log). It has to be made **before any other move**: nothing can be placed, discarded, locked or ended while a pick waits (the toast says "Choose an upgrade first."), and the run doesn't end with the pile empty and a pick waiting. **Undo steps back over the pick and the move that led to it together** (`undoCount`); a pick still pending goes back with that move alone. It replays, saves (`flowgrow.run.v7`, older ones are removed on load) and reloads like any other move, with the card back if a pick was waiting.
- **Several picks at once** (a Roots chain can do it, LOU-306): they wait in `S.pend` and are asked one at a time in reading order (row, then column), all before the next move. `needPick()` adds a tile to the list. Marsh stays automatic, with no pick.
- **A lock copy keeps its upgrade** (`f` on the tile, so copies are exact). Combined onto a habitat it adds nothing new (the habitat's own upgrade is what counts), planted at its own level (when the bare ground rule allows) it keeps it, and planted as level 1 under `"downgrade"` it has none, since level 1 is below the upgrade level. It offers no pick. The upgrade is **lost when the tile stops being prairie** (a tree overgrows it).
- **Steppe:** every time a steppe tile **levels up** (a combine onto it: to level 5, 6 and on), `steppePerLevel` (1) **free level 1 prairie** is planted on empty land beside it: not a mountain, a river tile or an occupied tile, and chosen by a fixed order, the six faces clockwise starting at the top right (NE, E, SE, SW, W, NW). Choosing the upgrade plants nothing. A free tile isn't taken from the pile, uses no tile and isn't counted as planted; it scores 1, plays as usual from then on and has no upgrade until it reaches level 4 itself. With no free tile nothing is planted and it tries again at the next level-up. `steppePerLevel: 6` fills every free slot and 0 plants none. It is part of the level-up, so undo takes the free tile away with it.
- **Meadow:** every time a meadow tile levels up, the points of that level-up are multiplied by **1 + the marsh and trees habitats beside it**, up to `meadowMax` (4). Neighbours count at any level, locked or not; green, prairie and river tiles don't. With none it is ×1. The level-up that reaches level 4 is not multiplied (there is no upgrade until it is picked), so the first multiplied one is to level 5. It is worked out from the board at that moment, so a replay gives the same result. The preview on the tile shows the multiplied points, the toast says "Meadow ×3: +18." and the run log "(Meadow ×3)". `meadowMax: 1` makes meadow score as usual.
- **Look:** two new entries in the look system (`UPG_LOOK`). **Steppe:** a red-orange rim and a mark of five seed heads fanning out (painted: seed heads fanning along the edges). **Meadow:** a magenta rim and a flower (painted: flowers in bloom along the edges). The tile waiting gets a gold ring and is panned up from under the card. A refused tap on a tile with an upgrade, and the pick itself, show that tile's line.

### Upgrades part 3: the trees' roots and pine-oneer, as built (Nov 21, LOU-306)

- **The pick for trees:** a **tree** habitat that first reaches the upgrade level (4) with no upgrade, by a combine, ~~by an **overgrow** that makes a level 4 tree,~~ *(overgrow was removed on Nov 22, LOU-314)* or by a **Roots lift** (below), has a pick to make: **Roots** or **Pine-oneer**. It is the pick of LOU-305, so it is stored on the tile, comes before any other move, undoes with what led to it, is kept by a lock's copy, and several at once wait in the list and are asked in reading order. No move turns a tree into another kind, so a tree never loses its upgrade; green gains nothing.
- **Roots:** every time a roots tree **levels up** (a combine onto it: to level 5, 6 and on), **every unlocked habitat that shares an edge with it and is lower than the tree rises one level for free**, each scoring the points for its new level like a combine. Reaching level 4 does nothing for roots: the first lift comes with the level-up to level 5. Dan: "put no cap on it", so it lifts every lower neighbour (`rootsPerLevel: 6`); 1 lifts only the lowest, 2 the two lowest, 0 none. Locked tiles, river tiles and bare ground don't rise, and neither does a tile equal to or above the tree. The toast says "Roots +4 tiles, 63 points." and the run log "(Roots lifted 4, 63 points)".
- **The chain** (`rootsChain: true`; Dan: "allow chain reaction for combos"): a tile that rises counts as having levelled up, so its own upgrade fires: a **steppe** plants its free tile, a **meadow** multiplies the points of the rise, a **reed bed** is a reed bed at its new level, and a **roots tree lifts its own lower neighbours** in turn. A tile that reaches level 4 this way has a pick to make, chain or not (so no tile sits at level 4 without an upgrade). With `rootsChain: false` they only rise and score. It is worked out as a queue in a fixed order (each tile's neighbours clockwise from the top right, first come first served), so a replay rebuilds the same run, and it is one move: undo takes back the whole chain. **It always ends:** a tile only rises while it is lower than the tree lifting it, so no level passes the level of the tree that started the chain (a packed board is one of the checks). A step limit of 20,000 sits in the code as a safety net only, and has never been reached.

A locked pine-oneer still counts (my reading, as for the reed bed), ~~and with `overgrow` off trees plant by reach as well as beside one. ### The opening trap, fixed (Nov 21)

- **The trap** (found while reading the Nov 21 simulations, LOU-308 suggestion 1; Dan: "fix the opening trap"): the pile opens with four river tiles, and the first option offered for the first one is to put it on the source, which raises the source to level 2 for 3 points ("wider +3"). From then on a level 1 river tile can't go anywhere, because a river tile can't be lower than the one it flows from, so the other three could only be discarded.
- **The fix** (`riverRaiseGuard`, on): a river tile **can't raise a river tile if that would leave another river tile waiting in the pile with no legal move** (no extend and no raise anywhere). Only river tiles in the pile the player can see count (the tile in hand and the next three), only raises are affected (every extend, plant, combine and lock is exactly as before), and the raise is simply not offered: its ring isn't drawn, and tapping the tile says "Extend the river first: raising it now would leave your other river tiles nowhere to go." Extending first leaves all four river tiles to place. **Nothing is lost:** a player who wants the raise discards the other river tiles first and then raises (checked: the same board, the same 3 points), because tiles that can never be placed are dead either way. With `riverRaiseGuard: false` the game is exactly what it was.

- **What Dan asked** (LOU-310 was written from his words): "create a ticket for "elevation" such that there is a "step down" on the tiles, this creates a water fall. what we'll do is that river tiles can 'step down" but for the land tiles there is no adjacency bonus on the step down. - **Separate maps, not a switch on the game.** The ticket put elevation behind `?elevation=true` on the usual game; Dan's message replaced that. **`elevation` is off in `flowgrow.config.js` and the usual game at `/` is unchanged**: with it off the simulator prints exactly what it printed before (12 settings, 500 runs each, byte for byte, against `9a45027`), and the ruleset key (so its best score) doesn't change either, because the elevation values join the key only when elevation is on. The maps are pages of their own: `/elevation/foothills/`, `/elevation/ridges/`, `/elevation/terraces/`, and a list at `/elevation/`. A map page is `index.html` with its own title, its paths two folders up and one script that switches elevation on and names the shape; the game's files are the same for every page. `tools/make-maps.mjs` writes the pages from `index.html` (`--check` says whether they match it, a rule check runs that), and they are in the service worker's list (v21). `?elevation=true&elevationShape=ridges` plays a map on the usual page for one visit, and `?stepAdjacency=all` on a map page plays the other step rule.
- **Each ruleset now has its own saved run** (`flowgrow.run.v12.<ruleset key>`; `run.v11` is dropped). Needed so that a map and the usual game don't overwrite each other's run (before, a saved run was one slot, and opening another ruleset started that one fresh). Best scores were already kept per ruleset.
- **The land:** with elevation on, every land cell (bare ground and the source's) has a height from 0 to `elevationLevels` (3). It is worked out from the **run's seed alone**, with a random stream of its own, so the pile is dealt exactly as it is with elevation off, nothing is saved, and every new run is new ground. Neighbouring land differs by at most 1 (a step is always one level), **the source is on the highest ground** and mountains count as the top height (the river never enters them). `elevOf(c, seed)` rebuilds it and keeps the last 64 seeds. The shape is `elevationShape`:
  - **foothills:** the height falls with the walking distance from the source (the island's radius split into the heights), plus bumps of one level on about 4 cells in 10, then low cells are raised to one below their highest neighbour. Bumpy: 48% of neighbouring land pairs are steps.
  - **ridges:** two or three ridges follow seeded paths out from the source and fall away at half the speed; land two or more cells from a ridge is a level lower. Valleys between ridges: 41% steps.
  - **terraces:** broad flat tiers fall away in one seeded direction; the source's side is a plateau at the top and each tier is a level lower. Few steps (17%) and long level stretches (68% of the land is at the top height).
- **Rivers:** a river tile put on empty land **can't be higher than the river tile it would flow from** (level or lower is fine), on every branch. "The tile behind it" is the one `riverMonotone` already uses (`joinTo`: the river's end if the cell touches one, otherwise the newest river tile it touches), shared, not copied; `riverMonotone` itself is unchanged. Raising a river tile, locking and reach are unchanged. The preview rings, `options()` and `why()` agree: a refused tap says "Rivers can't flow uphill: that land is height 3 and the river beside it is height 2." A river tile that **stepped down** from the tile it flows from is a **waterfall**; it is worked out from the heights and the river (nothing is stored), scores nothing and changes nothing else.
- **Across a step** (Dan: "for the land tiles there is no adjacency bonus on the step down"): one helper, `across(S, a, b, kind)`, says whether a step blocks a pair of land cells. `stepAdjacency` **"score"** (the ticket's default and the maps' setting): only Meadow's multiplier ignores marsh and trees across a step. **"all"** also stops Roots lifts, a Steppe's free tile, lock copies, prairie spread, reed beds and pine-oneers across a step. **"none"**: a step changes nothing here. A river tile is never blocked by it: **land beside a river counts as before, across a step too** (marsh planting beside a river, the marsh multiplier, a lock copying a river tile).
- **Look and words:** on the simple look empty land is tinted darker the lower it is, a dark line runs along the high side of every step and a waterfall is white lines across the edge the river stepped down at; on the painted look the land is lighter the higher it is, with the same dark lines and white lines (rough, as the ticket allows). A tap with nothing else to say gives "Height 2." and a river tile that stepped down says "Waterfall: the river steps down to height 2."; a refused tap on land adds its height. The menu says what the heights mean for that map. The page and menu carry the map's name.

- **The rule** (`greenTakeover: true`): a marsh, prairie or trees habitat of kind K and level L put on an **unlocked green habitat of level L** turns it into **kind K at level L + 1**. It scores the points for the new level, like a combine (a marsh tile times the river tiles touching it, like any marsh score), and the taker is used up. Different levels never take over, a locked green habitat can't be taken over, and river tiles and bare ground aren't green habitats (a tile on bare ground is a planting, as before). A lock's copy of a K tile takes over the same way. `greenTakeoverMaxLevel` (15, every level) is the highest green level that can be taken over (3: only low levels). - **It has to be a place the taker could be planted** (Dan's ticket), so green can't be used to skip the kind rules. `takeSpot` in `flowgrow.js` asks each kind's planting test of the green tile's cell as if it were empty: **marsh** only on green that shares an edge with a river tile or a reed bed (`marshNeedsRiver`); **prairie** only within the river's reach or beside prairie (`prairieSpreads`); **trees** only within reach or beside a pine-oneer. ### What is built (LOU-298)

### Locking, as built (LOU-299)

- **What can be locked:** any unlocked habitat or river tile, the source included (it counts as a level 1 river tile).
- **What a lock copies:** each neighbour that is an unlocked habitat or river tile at or below the locked tile's level. A higher neighbour gives nothing ("it's a yes/no on copies"). Empty land, mountains and locked tiles give nothing. A copy is exact: the neighbour's kind at its full level. The original stays on the map.
- **Where copies go:** to the back of the pile, behind every tile still to be dealt and behind copies banked earlier. They are kept as their own list, and "Tiles left" counts them.
- **A locked tile** can't be combined onto, widened, locked again or copied. It keeps its score and still counts as the top level for dealing.
- **Copies play like dealt tiles,** with the same rules and the same scoring. A level 2 river copy can only extend, and it reaches 2 tiles out.
- **Screen:** a **Lock** button in the bottom bar shows the locks held and the tiles until the next one (since Nov 20 there is no lock limit, so it shows ∞). Tapping it turns lock mode on, and tapping it again turns it off. In lock mode every lockable tile shows how many copies it would bank. Tapping one marks each copy on its neighbour, marks neighbours that are too high, and a card above the bar asks to confirm ("Lock this thicket?"). A locked habitat gets a padlock on its level pill and is painted a little muted. At the back of the pile panel, **Back of the pile** lists the banked copies in order. *Since Nov 22 (LOU-317) there is no Lock button: press and hold a tile (or right-click it) to lock it, and the card, the marks and the padlock are as here. See "The game screen, tidied".*
- **A lock is a move:** it can be undone, it is saved, and it has a line in the run log. The log marks each placed tile as dealt or copy. The result adds locks used and lost, and copies banked, placed and discarded, with the points from copies.
- **The six lock values** are in `flowgrow.config.js`: `lockEnabled`, `lockLevelCap`, `lockCopiesGo`, `lockRivers`, `lockEvery`, `lockHold`. With `lockEnabled: false` the game plays exactly as it did before locking.

Mine: the chain order is neighbours clockwise from the top right, first come first served; a locked pine-oneer still counts; a lifted tile that reaches level 4 has its pick even with the chain off; the toast and the marks are placeholders. **Flagged by the simulations:** `upgradeLevel` 4 is what keeps Roots in check. Lowering it makes Roots chains explode the best score (2,056 at level 3 and 3,443 at level 2, against about 1,000 at level 4, 61 to 69% of the points from Roots lifts), and a steppe snowballs the simple player's score (491 at level 2): see the numbers under "Upgrades part 3" and "Upgrades part 2".

- **What the numbers say, in short** (the full list and the ideas are in LOU-308; none of this changed a rule): a run's score is its lock copies (83% for the simple player, 82% for the best search run). - **The opening's trap** (found while reading these numbers): with the four-river opening the first tile in hand is a level 1 river tile, and the source is a river tile too, so the first option offered is to put it on the source: it raises the source to level 2 for **3 points** ("wider +3"). From then on a level 1 river tile can't be placed anywhere, because a river tile can't be lower than the one it flows from, so **the other three river tiles can only be discarded** (checked: after raising the source the options for the next river tile are none, and the toast says "A river tile can't be lower than the one it flows from (level 2)."). Extending first leaves every option open. The simple player falls into it (discards 4.6 a run against 2.1 with the old opening). Nothing was changed; Dan's options are listed in LOU-308.
